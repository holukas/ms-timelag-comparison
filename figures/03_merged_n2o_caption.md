**Figure 2.** Half-hourly N₂O fluxes in 2021 at the Chamau grassland site (CH-Cha, Switzerland) and the time lags used in the flux calculations. N₂O was measured with a quantum cascade laser (QCL, mini-QCLAS, Aerodyne Research Inc., MA, USA) from 1 Jan until 20 Jul and with a laser spectrometer (LGR, GLA351-N2OM1, Los Gatos Research, Mountain View, CA, USA) from 22 Jul until 31 Dec; the dashed line marks the instrument changeover and the dotted line a grassland renewal on 20 Aug. Letters along the top of the flux panels mark management events (F slurry fertilisation, H herbicide, S resowing, M mowing, T tillage and resowing, G sheep grazing), each with a thin grey line through the flux data. Grey bands above them mark rain periods. Fluxes are EddyPro output, before quality control. Columns: covariance maximisation without fallback (a, f, k), the same search falling back to the nominal lag (b, g, l), a narrow window with that fallback (c, h, m), a constant lag at the campaign nominal value (d, i, n), and pre-whitening with block-bootstrap cross-correlation (PWB<sup>OPT</sup>; Vitale et al., 2024) with 99 resamples (e, j, o). The lag search window differs between columns: 0 to 10 s in (a, b), a campaign-specific narrow window in (c) (0.40 to 0.90 s, default 0.60 s, for QCL; 1.50 to 3.30 s, default 1.75 s, for LGR), ±10 s in (e), and none in (d), where the lag is fixed. The wide-window run is exploratory: the modal lag of its distribution (k) sets the nominal lag of (b, c, d), its spread the window of (c). Rows: flux (top), lag used (middle), and the distribution of those lags per instrument in 0.05 s bins with the modal lag given (bottom). N₂O emissions alternate between extended low-flux periods and short episodes of high emission (top row, a–e), mainly after fertilisation (F) and the grassland renewal (T), so the signal available for temporal alignment varies strongly over the year. Without a fallback, the wide search accumulates lags at the window limits (f, k); the narrow search instead falls back to the nominal lag in a large share of half-hours (h, m), while the PWB<sup>OPT</sup> lags stay well inside their window (j, o). During the high-emission episodes, when the signal-to-noise ratio is high, the covariance-maximisation lags (f–h) agree more closely with the PWB<sup>OPT</sup> lag (j), although part of the wide-search lags remain at the window limits (f). From October onwards, when fluxes are low, the narrow-window lags spread across the window (h), whereas the PWB<sup>OPT</sup> lags remain in a narrow band (j). In periods where PWB<sup>OPT</sup> cannot detect a clear time lag, the optimal lag of the closest preceding averaging period is carried forward (j). The shared count axis is scaled to (o), so taller spikes are cut off. The equivalent figure for CH₄ is shown in the Supplement (Fig. S?).
